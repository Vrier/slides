Extensional, intensional and hyperintensional contexts

Definitions
Extension⟦φ⟧w: what an expression picks out at one world.
Intensionλw. ⟦φ⟧w: a function from worlds to extensions. For a sentence, the set of worlds where it is true.
Necessarily equivalentTrue at exactly the same worlds. The same intension.
Co-extensional, co-intensionalHaving the same extension at w; having the same intension.
Substitution salva veritateReplacing one expression by another without changing the truth value. Latin: with truth saved.
Extensional context⟦a⟧w = ⟦b⟧w ⇒ ⟦C[a]⟧w = ⟦C[b]⟧w. Co-extensional expressions can be swapped.
Intensional contextNot extensional. Some co-extensional pair cannot be swapped; co-intensional pairs still can.
Hyperintensional context□(A ↔ B) and H[A] ↮ H[B]: A and B are necessarily equivalent, and the two sentences still differ in truth value. Necessarily equivalent sentences cannot be swapped.

Example. Hesperus and Phosphorus both name Venus. Names are rigid, so both denote Venus at every world. Venus is bright at w1, w2 and w4.

  1. (1)Hesperus is bright.
  2. (2)Phosphorus is bright.

Circle the worlds where (1) is true, then draw a second outline round the worlds where (2) is true. The same worlds: (1) and (2) are necessarily equivalent. It is true that : extensional. (3) keeps its value when (1) is swapped for (2). Necessarily : intensional. (1) for (2) keeps the value; Hesperus is bright for Hesperus is a planet, co-extensional at w1, need not. Anna believes that : hyperintensional. Anna can believe (1) without believing (2).

  1. (3)It is true that Hesperus is bright.

Logical omniscience

Definitions
Doxastic alternativesDoxx(w): the worlds compatible with everything x believes in w. Intuition: the ways things could be, for all x believes.
believe⟦believe⟧w = λp. λx. Doxx(w) ⊆ p. The Hintikka entry.
Entailmentp entails q iff p ⊆ q: every p-world is a q-world.
Closure under entailmentDoxx(w) ⊆ p and p ⊆ q ⇒ Doxx(w) ⊆ q. Whoever believes p believes everything p entails.
Belief in all validitiesA validity is a sentence true at every world; it denotes W. Doxx(w) ⊆ W for every x, so everyone believes it.

Structured propositions

Definitions
Tuple⟨a, b, …⟩: an ordered list. ⟨a, b⟩ ≠ ⟨b, a⟩.
Structured propositionA tuple of the objects, properties and relations a sentence is about, arranged as the syntax arranges them.
Verb entryApplied to its argument, a verb returns a structure: ⟦flies⟧ = λo. ⟨Flying, ⟨o⟩⟩.
Connective entry⟦and⟧ = λq. λp. ⟨And, ⟨p, q⟩⟩.
Rigid nameA name that denotes the same individual at every world. ⟦Hesperus⟧ = ⟦Phosphorus⟧ = Venus at every world.
believe⟦believe⟧w = λσ. λx. σ ∈ Bx(w), where Bx(w) is the set of structures x believes in w.

Example. ⟦Sam flies⟧ = ⟦flies⟧(⟦Sam⟧) = ⟨Flying, ⟨Sam⟩⟩. ⟦Anna believes that Sam flies⟧w = 1 iff ⟨Flying, ⟨Sam⟩⟩ ∈ BAnna(w). The same belief iff the same tuple.

Situations

Definitions
SituationA part of a world. A world is a maximal situation, one with nothing left out.
Part ofs ≤ s′: everything in s is in s′.
Supports ⊨ φ: s makes φ true. A situation can be silent on φ, supporting neither φ nor its negation.
Perception reportx saw φ with a bare infinitive, as in saw Ben eat: true iff some situation x saw supports φ.

Truthmakers

Definitions
StateA part of a world, like a situation, that can also be impossible. Written s, t.
Fusions ⊔ t: the state made of s and t together. Any two states have one.
Impossible stateA fusion of states that cannot hold together, such as Cal eating chips fused with Cal eating no chips.
Exact verifications ⊩ φ: s makes φ true and all of s is relevant to doing so. Ben eating an ice cream exactly verifies Ben eats an ice cream; Ben eating an ice cream while it rains does not.
Conjunctions ⊩ A ∧ B iff s = t ⊔ u for some t ⊩ A and u ⊩ B. A verifier of each, fused.
Disjunctions ⊩ A ∨ B iff s ⊩ A or s ⊩ B. A verifier of either.
ContentThe set of exact verifiers of a sentence. Two sentences true at the same worlds can have different contents.

Compare support in section 4: a situation supports everything true in it, however much else it contains; a state exactly verifies only what it is wholly relevant to.

Kripke models for intuitionistic logic Bonus

Extra material, not needed for the assessment.

Definitions
StageA point k in a set K ordered by ≤, where some pairs may be unordered. Intuition: a state of information; k ≤ k′ means k′ extends k.
Forcingk ⊩ A: A is established at k. The model fixes this for atomic sentences.
Persistencek ⊩ A and k ≤ k′ ⇒ k′ ⊩ A. Once established, always established.
Conjunction, disjunctionk ⊩ A ∧ B iff k ⊩ A and k ⊩ B. k ⊩ A ∨ B iff k ⊩ A or k ⊩ B.
Negationk ⊩ ¬A iff no k′ ≥ k forces A. No extension of the information establishes A.
Implicationk ⊩ A → B iff every k′ ≥ k that forces A forces B.
ValidityForced at every stage of every model.

The order ≤ takes the place of the accessibility relation R. Two differences from a modal model: truth persists upward, and ¬ and → are evaluated at the later stages, as □ is at the accessible worlds. A stage is what has been proved so far; it need not settle every sentence.

Example. Stages k0 ≤ k1; p forced at k1 only. k0 ⊮ p. k0 ⊮ ¬p, since k1 forces p. k0 ⊩ ¬¬p, since no stage forces ¬p. So ¬¬p → p is not valid. Neither p nor ¬p at k0, so p ∨ ¬p is not valid either.
