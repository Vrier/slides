Module Overview

Semantics II

01

What the module is about

Semantics I built a theory of truth in one world: a model, denotations, predicate logic, λ and events. This module is about everything that theory cannot see. The Morning Star is the Evening Star was news, not a triviality; Sue thinks Harry is single is not settled by Harry's love life; Emma is seeking a unicorn is true with no unicorns anywhere. Semantics II rebuilds meaning across possible worlds — extension gives way to intension — and re-derives the operators that live above the verb: negation, modality, attitudes, conditionals, aspect, evidentiality.

The machinery is built early: worlds and modal logic in week 3, Kratzer's system in week 4; attitude reports, conditionals, aspect and evidentiality then reuse it rather than reinvent it, converging on the modals-and-tense capstone. Every teaching week runs four lenses: empirical → historical → formal → typological. Prerequisite: the Semantics I toolkit, recapped at speed in week 1.

Learning outcomes

On successful completion of this module, students will be able to:

  1. run the substitutivity diagnostic and identify opaque environments — attitudes, modals, epistemic adverbs, intensional transitives — in real language data;
  2. define and compute with the core intensional notions: extension vs intension, propositions as sets of worlds, world-relative interpretation, accessibility;
  3. analyse modal sentences in the Kripke and Kratzer frameworks (force, flavour, modal base, ordering source) and attitude reports in Hintikka's;
  4. apply the restrictor analysis of conditionals, the topic-time analysis of aspect, and the competing analyses of evidentials to new data;
  5. evaluate cross-linguistic evidence — variable-force modals, indexical shift, evidential systems — bearing on whether the intensional type inventory is universal.

Assessment

Three components · 10% + 30% + 60%

Assessment 1 · 10% — covers weeks 1 to 4: the extensional recap, the substitutivity diagnostics, worlds and modal logic, the Kratzer system. An early health-check, with feedback returned over the study week. Due Friday 16 October 2026 (week 5).

Assessment 2 · 30% — covers weeks 5 to 8: attitude reports, factivity and embedding, the restrictor analysis of conditionals. Due Friday 13 November 2026 (week 9).

Assessment 3 · 60% — the capstone component, covering the term with emphasis on weeks 8 to 12: conditionals, aspect, evidentiality, modals & tense. Due Monday 14 December 2026.

All three submitted via Blackboard. Titles, formats and briefs: released on Blackboard in advance of each deadline.
