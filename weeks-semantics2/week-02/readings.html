Week 2 · Readings

Worlds, Intensions & Modal Logic

Reading list Figures: C. I. Lewis · Carnap · Kripke

Four sources for Week 2, matching the lecture's readings slide — two textbook treatments of the possible-worlds machinery, the classical source behind the state-description slide, and an optional philosophical survey of what a world is — plus the paper the semantics comes from. References are in APA; open-access links are given where available. Read Portner first. Confirm editions and availability against the library / Blackboard list before circulating.

Reading list two textbook · two classical · one optional
  1. TextbookPortner, P. (2009). Modal logic. In Modality (chap. 2, §§2.1–2.2.3). Oxford University Press.
    The main text for this week: frames, models, truth at a world, validity, and the conditions on R — the same system as the lecture and Thursday's handout, in Portner's notation (see the handout's notation table).
    Library scan
  2. TextbookCoppock, E., & Champollion, L. (2026). Toward a theory of modality. In Invitation to formal semantics (§13.3) [Unpublished manuscript]. Boston University & New York University.
    The lexical entries for must and may as quantifiers over accessible worlds. Section numbers move between drafts (§12.3 in the September 2026 draft): go by the section title.
    Open access Open PDF
  3. ClassicalCarnap, R. (1947). Meaning and necessity: A study in semantics and modal logic (§2, §39). University of Chicago Press.
    State-descriptions (§2), with the gloss that they represent Leibniz's possible worlds, and necessity as truth in every state-description (§39) — the first detailed semantics for a modal operator.
    Library scan
  4. ClassicalKripke, S. A. (1963). Semantical considerations on modal logic. Acta Philosophica Fennica, 16, 83–94.
    Accessibility-relation semantics — the move that makes ◻ and ◇ quantifiers over worlds. Cited in the lecture; short, and readable after the handout.
    Purchase
  5. OptionalMenzel, C. (2016). Possible worlds. In E. N. Zalta (Ed.), The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (Winter 2016 ed.). Metaphysics Research Lab, Stanford University.
    §2 surveys what a world is — concretism, abstractionism, combinatorialism — behind the "Philosophy of possible worlds" slide.
    Open access SEP
