Doxastic alternatives

Definitions
Attitude holderThe subject of an attitude verb.
Doxastic alternativesDoxx(w): the worlds compatible with everything x believes in w. Intuition: the ways things could be, for all x believes.
Epistemic alternativesEpix(w): the worlds compatible with everything x knows in w.
Bouletic alternativesBulx(w): the worlds in which x’s desires in w are met.
Hintikka semanticsAn attitude verb is a universal quantifier over the attitude holder’s alternatives.
believe⟦believe⟧w = λp. λx. Doxx(w) ⊆ p
know⟦know⟧w = λp. λx. Epix(w) ⊆ p
want⟦want⟧w = λp. λx. Bulx(w) ⊆ p
Quantifier notationDoxx(w) ⊆ p iff ∀w′[w′ ∈ Doxx(w) → p(w′) = 1]. So ⟦believe⟧w = λp. λx. ∀w′[w′ ∈ Doxx(w) → p(w′) = 1], and likewise for know and want.

Axioms and closure

Conditions on Doxx(w) and Epix(w) decide which inferences about belief and knowledge come out valid.

Definitions
Alternatives as RFix x. Doxx(w) and Epix(w) are each an R(w).
Reflexivew ∈ R(w).
SerialR(w) ≠ ∅.
TransitiveIf w′ ∈ R(w) and w″ ∈ R(w′), then w″ ∈ R(w).
EuclideanIf w′ ∈ R(w) and w″ ∈ R(w), then w″ ∈ R(w′).
T, Dx knows p → p. x believes p → x does not believe ¬p.
4, 5Positive introspection: knows p → knows that knows p. Negative introspection: does not know p → knows that does not know p.
S4, KD45The usual systems for knowledge (T and 4) and for belief (D, 4 and 5).
ClosureIf x believes p and p ⊆ q, then x believes q.
Logical omnisciencex believes every necessary truth and everything entailed by what x believes.

Want

  1. (16)[Anna would rather not teach at all, but knows she has to] Anna wants to teach Tuesdays and Thursdays next semester.

(16) is true, although Anna’s desires are best met where she does not teach at all. Her desires rank the worlds she believes possible.

Definitions
Bouletic entryx wants p iff Bulx(w) ⊆ p, as in section 0.
Kratzerian entryModal base f: what x believes in w, so ∩f(w) = Doxx(w). Ordering source g: x’s desires in w. x wants p iff Best(f(w), g(w)) ⊆ p, or ∀w′[w′ ∈ Best(f(w), g(w)) → p(w′) = 1].

De se

Context. Perry follows a trail of sugar round a supermarket, looking for the shopper with the torn sack to tell him he is making a mess. The trail grows thicker on each lap. He is the shopper he is trying to catch.

  1. (18)[before he realises] Perry believes that the shopper with the torn sack is making a mess.
  2. (19)[after] I am making a mess.

(18) is true before and after. Only the belief in (19) makes him stop. For any description of himself, Perry could fail to know that he fits it.

Definitions
De dictoThe complement is a proposition, and any description in it is evaluated at the alternatives w′.
De reThe verb takes a res r and a property. x believes the property of r under some acquaintance relation A that x bears to r at w: seeing r, reading about r.
De seA belief about oneself, held as a belief about oneself. The verb takes a property and no res.
Centred worldA pair ⟨w′, y⟩ of a world and an individual in it, the centre. Intuition: a world with a marker saying who you are.
Centred alternativesDoxcx(w): the centred worlds ⟨w′, y⟩ compatible with what x believes in w, where y is the individual x would call I in w′.
Truth conditions
De dictox believes p iff ∀⟨w′, y⟩[⟨w′, y⟩ ∈ Doxcx(w) → p(w′) = 1]. The centre plays no role.
De rex believes P of r iff for some acquaintance relation A with Aw(x) = r, ∀⟨w′, y⟩[⟨w′, y⟩ ∈ Doxcx(w) → P(Aw′(y))(w′) = 1]. Aw′(y) is the individual y bears A to in w′.
De sex believes P de se iff ∀⟨w′, y⟩[⟨w′, y⟩ ∈ Doxcx(w) → P(y)(w′) = 1], equivalently Doxcx(w) ⊆ {⟨w′, y⟩ : P(y)(w′) = 1}.

Context. John, an amnesiac war hero, reads about the hero’s deeds and comes to expect that the hero will get a medal. He does not realise that he is the hero.

  1. (20)John expects that the hero will get a medal. de dicto
  1. (21)John expects that he will get a medal. de re
  1. (22)John expects to get a medal. de se

In the scenario (20) and (21) are true, and (22) is false. (21) and (22) share the verb, the individual, John, and the property, getting a medal. Only the de se report is false, so de se is neither de dicto nor de re. In the trees the difference is the complement: a proposition, a res plus a property, and a property alone. PRO is bound at the edge of the complement and has no res. The he of (21) can be bound the same way, which gives the sentence a de se reading too.

For Lewis every belief is the self-ascription of a property, and de dicto and de re belief are the special cases above. A context fixes a world and a speaker, so it works as a centred world. The shifted I of Amharic is read de se.

Interrogative and noun phrase complements

  1. (23)a. Anna knows [that Ben called].
    b. Anna knows [whether Ben called].
  2. (24)a. Anna believes [that Ben called].
    b. *Anna believes [whether Ben called].
  3. (25)a. *Anna wonders [that Ben called].
    b. Anna wonders [whether Ben called].
Definitions
Hamblin semanticsA question denotes the set of its possible answers. ⟦whether Ben called⟧ = {p, ¬p}, with p = ⟦that Ben called⟧. Type ⟨⟨s, t⟩, t⟩.
ResponsiveTakes declarative and interrogative complements: know.
RogativeTakes interrogative complements only: wonder.
Anti-rogativeTakes declarative complements only: believe.
know + questionx knows Q at w iff Epix(w) ⊆ p, for the p ∈ Q that is true at w.
Concealed questionA noun phrase read as a question: knows the capital of Italy as knows what the capital of Italy is.
Hidden clausewant a sloop as want to have a sloop; believe Mary as believe what Mary says.
Content nounA noun with a propositional content (rumour, story, claim). The verb applies to that content.
Modifier analysisThe clause modifies an argument of type e, a content-bearing individual, and the verb takes that argument.
