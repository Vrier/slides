Formal · Part A The extensional toolkit, on paper
Definition

A model \(M = \langle D, I\rangle\): a non-empty domain \(D\) and an interpretation function \(I\) taking constants to members of \(D\), one-place predicates to subsets of \(D\), two-place predicates to relations on \(D \times D\). An assignment \(g\) values the variables: \(\sem{u}^{M,g} = g(u)\).

Warm-up tool
Truth in a toy model

Work in the model from the lecture: \(D = \{n, a, d\}\) (Neil, Aoife, Dara); \(I(\textit{single}) = \{n, a\}\); \(I(\textit{tall}) = \{a, d\}\); \(I(\textit{admires}) = \{\langle n,a\rangle, \langle a,d\rangle\}\).

Practice · 4 tasks
1 Compute the truth value of Aoife is single and Dara is single. Justify each by checking membership.
2 Compute Neil admires Aoife \(\wedge\) \(\neg\)(Aoife is tall), showing the value of each conjunct first.
3 Is \(\sem{\exists x.\,\textit{single}(x) \wedge \textit{tall}(x)}^{M,g} = T\)? Name the assignment variant(s) \(g[x \mapsto k]\) that verify — or fail to verify — the scope.
4 State the truth condition of \(\forall x.\,\textit{tall}(x) \rightarrow \textit{single}(x)\), then evaluate it in \(M\).

Empirical Where extensional meaning runs out
New tool
Substitutivity as a diagnostic

Assume the Morning Star = the Evening Star (both Venus). Substituting co-referring expressions preserves truth in extensional (transparent) environments but can fail in intensional (opaque) ones. To diagnose an environment: swap co-referents and ask whether truth could flip.

Classify · 5 tasks
1 The Morning Star is a planet → The Evening Star is a planet. Transparent or opaque? Explain why the Semantics I system gets this one right.
2 Anna believes that the Morning Star is visible at dawn → Anna believes that the Evening Star is visible at dawn. Describe a scenario where the first is true and the second false.
3 The Empire State Building might be the tallest building in New York vs The Empire State Building might be One World Trade Center. What does the contrast show about might?
4 Little Emma thinks her house is the tallest building in New York. Can we conclude Little Emma thinks her house is One World Trade Center? Whose perspective fixes the reference of the description?
5 Stretch: is negation opaque? Test with The Morning Star is not visible tonight. What does your verdict tell us about which operators need worlds and which don't?
Data

Empty predicates: Jackalopes are fierce commits the speaker to jackalopes; Sue believes that jackalopes are fierce commits only Sue; Emma is seeking a unicorn commits nobody to unicorns.

Empty predicates
1Diagnose:

In an extensional semantics, \(\sem{\textit{jackalope}} = \emptyset = \sem{\textit{unicorn}}\). Explain precisely why this makes Sue believes jackalopes are fierce and Sue believes unicorns are fierce indistinguishable — and construct a scenario showing they must be distinguished.

Scenario

Quine's Ortcutt: Ralph sees a man in a trench coat in an alley and concludes "that man is a spy". He sees a man on the beach and concludes "that man is no spy". Unbeknownst to Ralph, both are Ortcutt.

De re and de dicto
1Judge:

Are (a) Ralph believes that the man in the alley is a spy and (b) Ralph believes that the man on the beach is not a spy both true in the scenario? What about (c) Ralph believes that Ortcutt is a spy and (d) Ralph believes that Ortcutt is not a spy? Say on which reading.

2Define, then apply:

Distinguish the de dicto and de re readings in your own words, then classify: (a) Ralph believes that there is a spy in the building; (b) Ralph believes of Ortcutt that he is a spy. Which construction forces which reading?

New tool
Modal flavour and strength

Flavour: which alternatives matter — epistemic (evidence), deontic (rules), circumstantial (facts and abilities), bouletic (desires), teleological (goals). Strength: necessity (must) vs possibility (might, can).

Sort · 4 tasks
1 Contrast At crosswalks all pedestrians obey the "Don't Walk" signals with At crosswalks all pedestrians must obey the "Don't Walk" signals. Which can be true while the other is false, and why?
2 Classify by flavour and strength: Daniel might be a secret agent; It is possible that Daniel is a secret agent; Maybe Daniel is a secret agent; Harry thinks that Daniel is a secret agent. Which of the four is not a modal at all — and what is it instead?
3 Give flavour and strength for: Visitors may use the car park; Aoife can swim; It must have rained overnight; You must try this cake.
4 Stretch: find one English sentence that is genuinely ambiguous between two flavours, and describe the two contexts that disambiguate it.
Historical Frege's puzzle to Montague — and its long prehistory
Background

The lecture traced a long arc: Aristotle's sea battle and the Stoics' modal classifications; Abelard's de dicto/de re; Scotus on synchronic contingency; Leibniz's possible worlds; then Frege (1892) on Sinn and Bedeutung, Carnap (1947) on intensions as functions from state-descriptions to extensions, Quine's resistance, and Montague's intensional fragment.

Reflect
1The open future:

Does there will be a sea battle tomorrow have a truth value tonight? Whatever you answer, spell out what it commits you to about bivalence and about tomorrow being "open".

2In your own words:

Why is \(a = a\) trivial while \(a = b\) can be news? State Frege's answer, and show how the same notion of Sinn explains why substitution fails under believe.

3Critical engagement:

Quine accepted that opacity exists but resisted quantifying into opaque contexts. Using the Ortcutt case, say what is troubling about \(\exists x.\,\textit{Ralph believes that } x \textit{ is a spy}\) — and why Carnap's intensions did not immediately satisfy him.

Formal · Part B From models to intensions
Definition

Extension: what an expression picks out at the world of evaluation. Intension: a function from worlds to extensions — \(\lambda w.\sem{\alpha}^{w}\). For sentences: the proposition \(\sem{S} = \{\,w : S \text{ is true at } w\,\}\), type \(\langle s,t\rangle\); truth at \(w\) is membership.

New tool
Extension / intension / type

For each expression give three things: its extension (at the actual world), its intension (as a function), and the type of that intension — e.g. the Taoiseach: the current officeholder; the function from each \(w\) to whoever heads the government at \(w\); \(\langle s,e\rangle\).

Practice · 3 tasks
1 the tallest building in New York — extension, intension, type. Then use your answer to explain why swapping it for a co-referring name under might flipped truth.
2 single — extension, intension, type. (Careful: the extension is already a set.)
3 Carlingford is in Northern Ireland — extension, intension, type. Why can you understand this sentence without knowing its extension?
New tool
Computing with worlds

Take four worlds. \(w_1\): rain in Dublin, Harry single. \(w_2\): rain, Harry married. \(w_3\): no rain, Harry single. \(w_4\): no rain, Harry married. Let \(p = \sem{\textit{it is raining in Dublin}}\) and \(q = \sem{\textit{Harry is single}}\).

Practice · 4 tasks
1 Write \(p\) and \(q\) as sets of worlds.
2 Compute \(p \cap q\) and \(\overline{p}\) (the complement), and say which sentence each set expresses.
3 Entailment is subset: does \(p \cap q\) entail \(p\)? Does \(p\) entail \(q\)? Show both using \(\subseteq\).
4 Suppose \(\mathrm{DOX}(\textit{Sue}) = \{w_1, w_2\}\). Does Sue believe \(p\)? Does she believe \(q\)? Apply the rule believe \(p\) iff \(\mathrm{DOX} \subseteq p\).
Stretch · thinking with DOX
1Limit cases:

What would it mean for \(\mathrm{DOX}(\textit{Sue})\) to be (a) the singleton containing only the actual world, (b) the set of all possible worlds, (c) empty? Describe the believer in each case.

2Back to the nesting problem:

Using types, state exactly why \(\sem{\textit{thinks}}\) cannot be \(\langle t,\langle e,t\rangle\rangle\), and what goes right once it is \(\langle\langle s,t\rangle,\langle e,t\rangle\rangle\). Illustrate with Sue thinks that Harry is single vs Sue thinks that Dublin is in Ireland.

Typological Is the type inventory universal?
Overview

English modals fix their strength in the lexicon (must = necessity, might = possibility) and leave flavour to context. St'át'imcets (Lillooet Salish) modals do the reverse: flavour is lexically fixed, force appears variable. The space of worlds may be universal; what grammar makes of it is not obviously so.

Cross-linguistic
1Your own data:

Take a language you know. How does it express epistemic must (It must have rained) versus deontic must (You must register)? Same form, different forms, or a construction that is not a modal verb at all?

2Predict:

If a language lexicalised flavour but not force, what substitution-style evidence would show that its "modals" still quantify over worlds? Sketch the test you would run — we run it for real in Week 4.
