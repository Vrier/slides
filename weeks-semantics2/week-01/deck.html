Part 1 · The system we built

The model

The model
\(M = \langle D, I\rangle\): a domain of individuals and an interpretation. Constants to members of \(D\); one-place predicates to subsets; two-place predicates to relations on \(D \times D\).
Assignments
\(g\) values the variables: \(\sem{u}^{M,g} = g(u)\). Everything else is fixed by \(I\).
The point
Denotation is always denotation in a model: a stand-in for the facts, one complete way for things to be.
A toy model
\[ D=\{n, a, d\} \qquad I(\textit{single})=\{n,a\} \qquad I(\textit{tall})=\{a,d\} \qquad I(\textit{admires})=\{\langle n,a\rangle, \langle a,d\rangle\} \]
Neil, Anna and Dara: our domain for the hour
Part 1 · The system we built

Truth in the model

At the bottom
Predication is membership: a sentence is true when the named things stand in the named set or relation.
Going up
The connectives see only the parts' truth values. Nothing else about the parts survives.
The recursive clauses
\[ \sem{\pi(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)}^{M,g} = T \;\text{ iff }\; \langle\sem{\alpha_1}^{M,g},\dots,\sem{\alpha_n}^{M,g}\rangle \in \sem{\pi}^{M,g} \]
\[ \sem{\neg\varphi} = T \text{ iff } \sem{\varphi} = F \qquad \sem{\varphi \wedge \psi} = T \text{ iff } \sem{\varphi} = T \text{ and } \sem{\psi} = T \]
in the toy model: ⟦Neil is single⟧ = T, since n ∈ I(single)
Part 1 · The system we built

Quantifiers and assignments

The trick
An assignment variant \(g[u \mapsto k]\) is \(g\) with the variable \(u\) re-pointed at \(k\). Quantifiers survey the variants.
The two clauses
\[ \sem{\exists u.\varphi}^{M,g} = T \;\text{ iff for some } k \in D:\; \sem{\varphi}^{M,g[u\mapsto k]} = T \]
\[ \sem{\forall u.\varphi}^{M,g} = T \;\text{ iff for all } k \in D:\; \sem{\varphi}^{M,g[u\mapsto k]} = T \]
in the toy model: ∃x(single(x) ∧ tall(x)), verified by g[x ↦ a]

Types, λ and Function Application

Types
Two basic types, \(e\) and \(t\), and functions \(\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle\) between them: predicates \(\langle e,t\rangle\), transitives \(\langle e,\langle e,t\rangle\rangle\), a sentence \(t\).
Two rules
Function Application, where the types say which sister is the function. Predicate Modification, where both sisters are \(\langle e,t\rangle\).
The two rules
\[ \sem{\beta\;\gamma} = \sem{\beta}\left(\sem{\gamma}\right) \qquad\qquad \sem{\beta\;\gamma} = \lambda x.\,\sem{\beta}(x) \wedge \sem{\gamma}(x) \]
Function Application, and Predicate Modification for two \(\langle e,t\rangle\) sisters

Events and times

Davidson
Verbs describe events; sentences quantify over them; adverbs are predicates of the event variable. Modification is conjunction.
Tense
Times came last: tense locates the event relative to now. Events and times enlarged the ontology. Every claim was still checked against one world's facts.
The Davidsonian move
(1)Neil giggled loudly.⇝ \(\exists e\,(\textit{giggle}(e,n) \wedge \textit{loud}(e) \wedge e \prec \textit{now})\); drop a conjunct and Neil giggled follows for free

What Part 1 gives the complementWhat would follow
A truth value
\(\sem{\textit{Harry is single}} = T\). The clause hands thinks the value T, and nothing else.
Disaster
Any true complement becomes interchangeable; believing one truth means believing them all.

Many more puzzles and problems

Where the extensional analysis breaks down.
Decide: is (5) as trivial as (4)
(4)
The Morning Star is the Morning Star.
(5)
The Morning Star is the Evening Star.
Both name Venus.

Frege's Puzzle

The facts
The Morning Star and the Evening Star pick out one object. If meaning were reference, (4) and (5) would feel exactly alike.
The asymmetry
They don't. \(a=a\) is trivial; \(a=b\) is informative.
Empirical

Leibniz's Law

Swap co-referring terms
(6)The Morning Star is a planet.true
(7)The Evening Star is a planet.true; the same verdict is guaranteed, since the two terms co-refer
The law
Substituting co-referring expressions preserves truth: salva veritate. Environments where this holds are extensional, or transparent.
A theorem of the Semantics I system. If all ⟦·⟧ tracks is extension, sentences differing in co-extensional parts cannot differ in truth value.
Anna failed astronomy. Run the same swap
(8)
Anna believes that the Morning Star is visible at dawn.
(9)
Anna believes that the Evening Star is visible at dawn.
Empirical

Opacity

(8), in Anna's scenario(9), after the swap
True
She watches it every morning and says so.
Can be false

Modals misbehave the same way
(10)The Empire State Building might be the tallest building in New York.a sensible thing to wonder
(11)#The Empire State Building might be One World Trade Center.absurd, although the two descriptions co-refer if (10)'s guess is right
(12)Little Emma thinks her house is the tallest building in New York.⇏ Little Emma thinks her house is One World Trade Center
The diagnostic
Same extensions, different truth values: these environments are opaque. Substitutivity is the field's first test for intensionality.
Empirical

The opaque environments

Attitude verbsalternatives: someone's beliefs
Harry is single: checkable against the facts. Sue thinks that Harry is single: true or false whatever Harry's love life.
Modalsalternatives: rules, facts, goals, evidence
Every car is registered vs every car must be registered; the second can be true while the first is false. The form stays constant; the flavour (rules, evidence, goals) and the force (must vs might) vary. Weeks 2 and 3.
Epistemic adverbsalternatives: what is known
Possibly, Hannah is in the library. Where Hannah actually is does not settle it.
Intensional transitivesno actual-world witness needed
Emma is seeking a unicorn: true this morning, unicorns or no unicorns.

A former senator is not a senator. Disjoint from the noun's extension.

The difference
Intensionality inside modification. No operator over a clause. Montague (1970).

Empirical

Empty predicates

Who is committed to what
(13)Jackalopes are fierce.commits me to jackalopes
(14)Sue believes that jackalopes are fierce.commits Sue; I am off the hook
(15)Emma is seeking a unicorn.commits nobody to unicorns
The shift
Embedding moves existential commitment from the speaker to the attitude-holder. Referents inside the complement answer to the attitude.
Extensionally, \(\sem{\textit{jackalope}} = \emptyset = \sem{\textit{unicorn}}\). An extensional system cannot distinguish believing in jackalopes from believing in unicorns.
