The imperfective paradox

Definitions
StateNo change. It holds at every moment while it lasts. own a car
ActivityEvery part of the event is an event of the same kind. push a cart
AccomplishmentThe event has an endpoint. A part of it is not an event of the same kind. draw a circle
Imperfective paradoxThe progressive of an accomplishment does not entail its simple past.
  1. (7)John was pushing a cart. ⇒ John pushed a cart.
  2. (8)John was drawing a circle. ⇏ John drew a circle.
Example (8)
  1. (8) with IPFV, tense left aside. ∃e∃x [draw(e) ∧ agent(e, j) ∧ circle(x) ∧ theme(e, x) ∧ T ⊆ E]
  2. John drew a circle, tense and aspect left aside. ∃e∃x [draw(e) ∧ agent(e, j) ∧ circle(x) ∧ theme(e, x)]
  3. Line 1 is line 2 with one more conjunct, so (8) is predicted to entail John drew a circle.
  4. But (8) is true if John stops halfway, and then no circle is ever drawn.

The progressive as a modal

On Portner’s analysis the progressive is a modal with the shape of must. (8) says that in the best worlds, the drawing John has started goes on to a complete circle.

Definitions
Modal baseCirc(e, P): the circumstances of e. ∩Circ(e, P): the worlds where they all hold.
Ordering sourceNI(e): propositions saying that e is not interrupted.
Betterw′ is better than w″ if w′ makes true every proposition of NI(e) that w″ does, and more.
BestBest(Circ(e, P), NI(e)): the worlds of ∩Circ(e, P) with no better world.
PROGPROG(e, P) is true at w iff in every world of Best(Circ(e, P), NI(e)), e goes on to a complete P event.
IPFVmod⟦IPFVmod⟧ = λP. λt. ∃e [t ⊆ E ∧ PROG(e, P)]. The English progressive.
mustmust φ is true at w iff φ is true in every world of Best(f(w), g(w)). PROG has the same shape.

Example (16)
  1. w1 and w2 make n1 and n2 true. w0 and w3 make one each. Best(Circ(e, P), NI(e)) = {w1, w2}.
  2. Max completes the crossing in w1 and in w2. PROG(e, P) is true at w0.
  3. w0 is not a best world, so (16) does not entail that Max crossed the street.

  2. ⟦TP⟧ says that an event e is going on at T, before S, and that in every world of Best(Circ(e, P), NI(e)) e goes on to a complete drawing of a circle by John.
