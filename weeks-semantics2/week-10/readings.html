Week 10 · Readings

Aspect & the Perfect

Reading list Figures: Vendler · Dowty · Landman · Klein · Portner

Five sources for Week 10: a textbook reading on aspect and on the perfect, two classical papers (the imperfective paradox and the present perfect puzzle), and two contemporary papers (the progressive as a modal and the perfect time span). References are in APA; open-access links are given where available. Read Kroeger first, then Dowty.

Reading list one textbook · two classical · two contemporary
  1. TextbookKroeger, P. R. (2022). Aspect and Aktionsart; Varieties of the perfect. In Analyzing meaning: An introduction to semantics and pragmatics (3rd ed., chaps. 20 and 22). Language Science Press.
    Grammatical aspect and its interaction with the aspectual classes; the readings of the perfect and the present perfect puzzle.
    Open access Open PDF
  2. ClassicalDowty, D. R. (1977). Toward a semantic analysis of verb aspect and the English ‘imperfective’ progressive. Linguistics and Philosophy, 1(1), 45–77. https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00351936
    The imperfective paradox and the first modal analysis of the progressive.
    Library login DOI
  3. ClassicalKlein, W. (1992). The present perfect puzzle. Language, 68(3), 525–552.
    Topic time, and why the English present perfect rejects definite past adverbials.
    Library login Cambridge Core
  4. ContemporaryPortner, P. (1998). The progressive in modal semantics. Language, 74(4), 760–787. https://doi.org/10.2307/417002
    The progressive with a circumstantial modal base and a non-interruption ordering source.
    Library login DOI
  5. ContemporaryIatridou, S., Anagnostopoulou, E., & Izvorski, R. (2001). Observations about the form and meaning of the perfect. In M. Kenstowicz (Ed.), Ken Hale: A life in language (pp. 189–238). MIT Press.
    The perfect time span, and universal and existential perfects.
    Library login
