Games and equilibrium

Definitions
Normal-form gamePlayers; for each a set of actions; for each a utility function from action profiles to numbers.
Best responseA strategy not beaten by any other strategy.
Nash equilibriumEvery choice of every player is a best response to the others such that nobody can gain by deviating alone. Strict iff every lone deviation is strictly worse.
Pareto dominanceo′ dominates o: at least one player better off, nobody worse.
Pareto optimalDominated by nothing.
Zero-sum gamePayoffs sum to zero in every cell, such that any player can only gain by someone else losing.
Solution conceptA rule selecting behaviour from a game model. Nash equilibrium is one; iterated best response, below, is another.

Convention as equilibrium

A semantic convention: arbitrary, stable, no founding agreement. A self-sustaining solution to a coordination problem, found here by enumeration.

Definitions
Signalling game⟨S, M, A, u⟩. Nature picks a state, either may come up; the sender sees it and sends a message; the receiver sees only the message and acts; both get u(s, a).
Pure strategiesσ: states → messages. ρ: messages → acts.
Success scoreSc(σ, ρ) = |{s : ρ(σ(s)) fits s}|: 0, 1 or 2.

Lewis 1969. Convention. Harvard University Press. Ch. IV.

Definitions
Payoffs+2 if the receiver gets the right referent; +1 for a one-word signal; −5 if she gets it wrong or lacks the information to choose.
Iterated best responseLevel-0: salient, unstrategic behaviour. Level-(k+1): a best response to level-k. Fixed points are Nash equilibria, so the ladder reaches equilibrium by reasoning.
Literal hearerApplies the dictionary: picks the referent if the signal's denotation is a singleton; otherwise stuck.
Literal speakerTruthful; picks her best signal against the literal hearer.
Pragmatic hearerRules out referents whose speaker had a strictly better signal.
Pragmatic speakerTruthful; picks her best signal against the pragmatic hearer.

Scalar implicatures

States: none passed (n), some but not all (sna), all passed (sa). Signals and denotations:

Definitions
Message costLonger, less lexicalised forms cost more to produce; a play's payoff is docked by its message's cost.
No wasted costsA speaker pays more only for compensation: a costly surprise message signals the cheap one would have misled.
