Game Theory and Signalling

Reading list Figures: Grice · Sperber & Wilson · Clark

Three sources for Week 2: a textbook introduction to game theory for linguists, the classical account of convention, and a contemporary survey of game-theoretic pragmatics. References are in APA; open-access links are given where available. Confirm editions and availability against the library / Blackboard list before circulating.

Reading list one textbook · one classical · one contemporary
  1. TextbookBenz, A., Jäger, G., & van Rooij, R. (2006). An introduction to game theory for linguists. In A. Benz, G. Jäger, & R. van Rooij (Eds.), Game theory and pragmatics (pp. 1–82). Palgrave Macmillan. https://doi.org/10.1057/9780230285897_1
    Games, strategies, equilibria and signalling from first principles, written for linguists.
    Library
  2. ClassicalLewis, D. (1969). Convention: A philosophical study. Harvard University Press.
    Convention as a coordination equilibrium, and signalling as a convention.
    Purchase
  3. ContemporaryFranke, M. (2013). Game theoretic pragmatics. Philosophy Compass, 8(3), 269–284. https://doi.org/10.1111/phc3.12015
    A survey of game-theoretic pragmatics.
    Library
