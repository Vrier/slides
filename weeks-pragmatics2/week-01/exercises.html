Three short problems. They set up the two specimens you will carry through the whole term, and they ask you to notice the strain in the classical machinery before we start replacing it. Short answers throughout; none of this is marked.

1 The derivation, taken apart

Write out the route from Some of the students passed to not all of them passed as a numbered argument, with every premise stated separately.

Then: (a) identify the premise concerning what the speaker knows, (b) say what the argument yields if you delete it, and (c) say where that premise came from.

2 A diagnostics table

Build a small table. Rows: the scalar inference from some, and the inference from Alex stopped smoking that Alex used to smoke. Columns: survives negation, survives questioning, cancels without contradiction.

Fill it in with examples rather than ticks, then say in one sentence what the pattern shows.

3 The problem to bring to Week 2

The derivation in problem 1 was presented as valid. Its conclusion cancels. In a short paragraph, set out why those two facts sit badly together, and sketch one way out that does not involve giving up on the derivation altogether.

Any answer is fine here. The point is to have a position before the next session argues for one.
