This week

Session 1. Recap of the prior pragmatics course: implicature, speech acts, presupposition. Reframed around one question: was Grice doing inference, or describing a code?

Session 2. The three-cornered space, the two diagnostics, and the interface as the running theme. The course question: does mastering statistical structure deliver grammar and inference for free?

The three approaches

Inference. Meaning reconstructed from speaker intentions. Grice, Relevance Theory, RSA.

Grammar. Meaning encoded and computed by the linguistic system, through covert operators and conventionalisation. Chierchia, Potts, Levinson.

Information. Meaning carried by the statistics of the signal: information, surprisal, efficiency, with large language models as the limit case. Shannon, Jaeger, Frank and Goodman.

The two diagnostics

Two specimens run through every corner of the course. Scalar implicature: Some of the students passed, heard as not all of them did. Presupposition: Alex stopped smoking, conveying that Alex used to smoke even under negation.

Every approach has to derive both. Where the derivations come apart, the approaches become testable against each other.

New tool
The three-corner test

Take any pragmatic inference and ask three questions of it. Does the hearer reason to it from what a cooperative speaker would have said? Does the grammar compute it through a silent operator? Does it fall out of the statistics of which forms get chosen? Each answer predicts something different about when the inference appears and how fast it arrives.

Practice · 3 tasks
1 State the scalar implicature of Some of the students passed, then show it is cancellable by writing a continuation that removes it without contradiction.
2 Apply the negation test to Alex stopped smoking. Give the negated sentence, say what each version asserts, and identify the content that survives.
3 Cancellability separates the two diagnostics. Attempt the same cancellation move on the presupposition in task 2 and record what goes wrong.
Progression · deriving one inference three ways
The same datum throughout: Some of the students passed. Sketch the derivation each corner would give. Two or three sentences each.
(a)

Inference. Use the scale and what a cooperative speaker who knew more would have chosen.

(b)

Grammar. Use a silent operator roughly equivalent to only, applying in the syntax.

(c)

Information. Use a listener who models a speaker choosing between some and all, and reasons about which was more likely to be uttered.

Standalone tasks
1In your own words:

Explain what would have to be true for Grice to count as describing a code rather than a pattern of reasoning.

2Take a position:

The course question asks whether mastering statistical structure delivers grammar and inference for free. Name one piece of evidence that would push you towards yes, and one that would push you towards no.
