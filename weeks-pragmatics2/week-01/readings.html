Week 1 · Readings

Three Roads to Meaning

Reading list Figures: Grice · Sperber & Wilson · Levinson

Primary-reading-led. Week 1 revisits the source of the inferential picture, then sets the cognitive reframing against it. Read the classical piece first, the contemporary chapter second, and use the textbook introduction as a light recap of the field's shape. Tight excerpting is expected for the harder primary texts.

Reading list one classical · one contemporary · one textbook / secondary
  1. ClassicalGrice, H. P. (1975). Logic and conversation. In P. Cole & J. L. Morgan (Eds.), Syntax and Semantics 3: Speech Acts (pp. 41–58). Academic Press.
    Revisited, with a question attached: read it as an account of how hearers reason, then read it again as a description of a code. The course turns on which reading survives.
  2. ContemporarySperber, D., & Wilson, D. (1995). Relevance: Communication and Cognition (2nd ed.), ch. 1. Blackwell.
    The cognitive reframing of Grice: ostensive-inferential communication, cognitive effects weighed against processing effort. Track where it departs from the maxims.
  3. TextbookLevinson, S. C. (1983). Pragmatics, introduction. Cambridge University Press.
    Light recap. Useful for the scope of the field and for the semantics and pragmatics boundary, which runs as this term's standing thread.
