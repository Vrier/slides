Session 1 · start here

Two sentences

Some of the students passed.
Alex stopped smoking.
What the room reports
The first conveys that not all of them passed. The second conveys that Alex used to smoke. Neither sentence says either thing.
Two properties worth noticing
Nobody had to be told. Nobody had to work at it. Both inferences arrived fast and cost almost nothing.
These two run the term
Scalar implicature and presupposition are the standing specimens of this course. Every account you meet has to handle both.
Session 1 · specimen one

Scalar implicature

Uttered
Some of the students passed.
Alternative available
All of the students passed.
Reasoning
A speaker who knew all would have said all.
Inferred
Not all of them passed.
Cancellable
Some of the students passed. In fact I have no idea how many. No contradiction. The inference can be withdrawn; what was said cannot.
Session 1 · specimen two

Presupposition

Positive
Alex stopped smoking.
Asserts
Alex does not smoke now.
Negated
Alex didn't stop smoking.
Survives
Alex used to smoke.
Try the cancellation move
Alex didn't stop smoking. In fact Alex never smoked. Something has gone wrong that did not go wrong a slide ago.
Session 1 · the asymmetry

The two specimens behave differently

Under negation
The implicature disappears. The presupposition survives.
Under cancellation
The implicature goes quietly. The presupposition resists.
In the discourse
The implicature is computed and added. The presupposition is taken for granted before the sentence starts, which is to say it was already probable.
Why this matters
Both count as meaning conveyed without being said, and they come apart under every test. Any account that explains one has to earn the other.
Session 1 · the reframing

Grice reread

Was Grice doing inference, or describing a code?
As reasoning
The hearer computes it live, about this speaker, on this occasion. The derivation on the earlier slide is a description of something the mind does.
As a regularity
The pattern is stable enough to be stored and looked up. The derivation is a rational reconstruction of something that no longer needs computing.
The question is decidable
The readings predict different things: how fast the inference arrives, whether it survives embedding, how much it costs the hearer. Those are measurable.
Grice's text supports both
He was never asked to choose. You will be.
Session 1 · in passing

Speech Acts

I now pronounce you husband and wife.
The Pragmatics I treatment
Said by the right person in the right setting, the utterance performs the marriage. Felicity conditions replace truth conditions.
Why it is not a diagnostic here
It yields no single inference that rival accounts can derive in competing ways, so it cannot referee between them. It returns later as a presentation topic: whether a system can assert, promise, or be held to what it says.

Session 2 · the argument of the course

Comprehension as inference under resource limits

Three of the frameworks in this course are saying something close to the same thing, in different notation.
Relevance Theory · week 2
Comprehension maximises cognitive effects against processing effort. Interpretation stops when the return no longer justifies the cost.
Rational Speech Acts · week 8
The listener inverts a model of a speaker choosing rationally among alternatives. Bayes' rule turns the speaker model into a posterior over meanings.
Information theory · week 6
Surprisal, \(-\log_2 p\), measures how unexpected a signal is. Cost in bits, and speakers smooth that cost across an utterance.
The claim underneath
Effort, probability and surprisal are different notations for the same thought: understanding is inference performed by a mind with limited resources, and the limit is measurable.
Session 2 · where the evidence comes from

Cognitive science as the connective tissue

Processing
Reading times and response latencies put numbers on effort. If an inference is computed live it should cost time; if it is stored it should not.
Prediction
Comprehenders anticipate what comes next. Surprisal predicts how long they dwell when the signal defeats the prediction.
Language models as instruments
Next-token prediction yields a surprisal estimate for any text. That makes a model a measuring device for the theory, before it is ever a rival to it.
This is the hook of the course
The cognitive story, the Bayesian machinery and the information-theoretic measure arrive together. Holding them together is what the term is for.
Session 2 · the rival

Grammar as the contrast case

The alternative
A silent operator, roughly only, applies in the syntax. The strengthened reading is computed by the grammar, with no reasoning about speakers at all.
Why it is a serious rival
Implicatures appear embedded under quantifiers and attitude verbs, where reasoning about the whole utterance should not reach. Weeks 4 and 5.
What it does for us
It keeps the probabilistic story honest. A picture that explains everything explains nothing, so the grammatical account is the thing we test against.
Session 2 · the running thread

The semantics and pragmatics interface

How much inference intrudes into truth-conditional content?
A thread, never a week
Flagged wherever a derivation forces the question. It gets no session of its own.
Where it bites
Embedded implicatures, presupposition projection, and the status of enriched content.
A moving border
The boundary shifts as theories improve. Several phenomena once filed under pragmatics now have strong semantic analyses.
Session 2 · the question behind the term

The course question

Does mastering statistical structure deliver grammar and/or inference for free?
The and/or is doing work
The question has three possible answers. Statistics might deliver both, or either one on its own, and each result says something different about what pragmatics needs as a primitive.
Why it is live now
Next-token prediction is a statistical code at scale. Systems trained on nothing else handle a good deal of what this course studies.
What would count as an answer
Evidence about whether such a system handles both specimens the way people do, at the same cost, with the same failures.
Where you come in
Weeks 9–12 are your presentations, drawn from a pool that extends exactly these questions.
