The speaker

Definitions
Surprisal leftlog2 1L0(s | u) bits. How far she still is from certainty about s.
CostC(u), the effort of producing u, in bits. Taken as 0 here, where every signal is a single word.
UtilityU(u; s) = log2 L0(s | u) − C(u). Minus the surprisal left, minus the cost. Never above 0.
SpeakerS1(u | s), his probability of choosing u in state s: its score, 2α · U(u; s), divided by the total over every utterance u′. A soft-max: the best utterance gets the largest share, the others keep one.
Rationality αEach extra bit of utility multiplies an utterance’s chance by 2α. 0 random among true utterances; 1 twice as likely per bit; large, always the best. With no costs a score is L0(s | u)α.

Costs

Definitions
Two-word signalblue square, green square, blue circle, green circle. A colour word and a shape word, true of one object only.
Cost of a signalC(u) = 1 bit for each word after the first. blue circle costs 1 bit.
Score with a cost2α · U(u; s), which is L0(s | u)α × 2−α · C(u).
Best signalThe true signal with the highest utility, so the one the speaker chooses most often.

What you need to know

Concepts
  1. The literal listener.
  2. The speaker’s utility; informativity and cost.
  3. The soft-max choice rule and the parameter α.
  4. The pragmatic listener.
  5. Levels of reasoning.
Skills
  1. Compute what a literal listener believes from a prior and a word’s meaning.
  2. Compute a speaker’s choices by scoring each true utterance and normalising.
  3. Raise a distribution to a power and normalise.
  4. Compute a pragmatic listener’s beliefs with Bayes’ rule.
  5. Derive a scalar implicature with numbers.
  6. Read L0, S1, L1, α and C in the literature.
