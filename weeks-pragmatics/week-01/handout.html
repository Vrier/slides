Pragmatics is the study of how hearers get from what is said to what is meant — systematically, not by magic.

New tool
Said vs meant

The working distinction of the whole module. For every datum, ask three questions: what was literally said? what was meant? and by what route did the hearer get from one to the other? The grammar alone underdetermines what an utterance does.

Historical From the ideal-language dream to language in use
Background

The spine. Frege (Begriffsschrift, 1879; sense vs reference — Hesperus and Phosphorus name one planet yet differ in meaning), Russell (On Denoting, 1905; grammatical form misleads — logical form is where meaning lives) and the early Wittgenstein (Tractatus, 1921; a sentence pictures a possible fact) make meaning a matter of reference, truth and logical form — pushing speakers, hearers and contexts out of the picture.

Positivism radicalises this: for the Vienna Circle (Schlick, Neurath, Carnap) and Ayer (Language, Truth and Logic, 1936), a sentence's meaning is its method of verification, and what cannot be verified is strictly meaningless. Ordinary language philosophy strikes back: the late Wittgenstein (“the meaning of a word is its use in the language”, PI §43), Austin (performatives — felicitous or misfired, not true or false) and Strawson (presupposition, against Russell) find orderly use where positivism saw defective logic. Grice then bridges the camps: speaker meaning as intention recognition, cooperation and implicature — language is logical, and use goes beyond logic, systematically.

Showpiece

Russell 1905 — The king of France is bald, made logical:

\[ \exists x\,\bigl(F(x) \;\land\; \forall y\,(F(y)\rightarrow y=x) \;\land\; G(x)\bigr) \]

Existence, uniqueness, predication — no mysterious reference to a non-existent king. The showpiece of the ideal-language method: messy natural language replaced by pure logic.

Reflect
1The positivist verdict:

On the verification doctrine, most everyday talk — evaluation, ritual, indirectness, small talk on footpaths — comes out strictly meaningless. Explain how the doctrine delivers that verdict, and why the footpath exchange is a problem for it.

2Strawson against Russell:

For Russell, The king of France is bald is simply false. What does Strawson say the sentence does instead, and which everyday intuition about “taking for granted” does that capture?

3The two Wittgensteins:

The early Wittgenstein inspires the positivists' ideal language; the late Wittgenstein helps found the rebellion against it. Give the one-line doctrine of each.

Formal Semantics, pragmatics, and four contemporary programmes
Definition

Semantics — meaning contributed by linguistic expressions: what the words and their combination encode. Pragmatics — meaning contributed by use and inference in context: what is taken for granted, what the speaker is trying to achieve, what counts as a relevant move.

The boundary between them is a live research question — some “pragmatic” phenomena now have strong semantic analyses, and the border shifts as theories improve. It is where this course ends.

Applied Appropriateness, indeterminacy, reflexivity — and the project
Overview

Three observations from the footpath. Appropriateness: every utterance is fitted to its moment — to who may speak, to whom, about what — and that fit, not just truth, is what speakers are judged on. Indeterminacy: what an utterance means is often negotiated after the fact — the mother's It certainly does settles what the platitude was doing. Reflexivity: language comments on its own use constantly — hedges, echoes, scare quotes.

The term project documents these in the wild: choose a phenomenon and a setting where it lives, collect a small dataset of real use (transcripts, excerpts, a mini-corpus — not invented examples), analyse week by week as the tools arrive, and write it up research-paper style.

Toward the project
1Project seed:

Choose a candidate phenomenon (implicature, presupposition, politeness…) and a speech event or interaction type where it lives. One sentence each for Choose / Collect / Analyse: what phenomenon, what setting, what data you could gather.

2The footpath revisited:
