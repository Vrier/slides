What the module is about

Pragmatics is the study of meaning in context: how speakers use language to communicate, and how hearers interpret what they say. Its central observation is that the meaning conveyed by an utterance regularly goes beyond the conventional meaning of the sentence uttered. The additional content is recovered from the context of use and from general principles of communication. Semantics studies the conventional meaning of expressions; pragmatics studies the contribution of use and context. The boundary between the two is a live research question.

Weeks 1 to 8 cover some core Pragmatic phenomena: speech acts, implicature, presupposition, common ground, information, politeness. Weeks 9 to 12 introduce four contemporary programmes in modern Pragmatics: Neo-Gricean, Relevance Theory, Rational Speech Acts, Grammaticalism.

Learning outcomes

On successful completion of this module, students will be able to:

  1. identify and analyse the core phenomena (speech acts, implicature, presupposition, common ground and accommodation, information structure, politeness) in real language use;
  2. run diagnostics: felicity vs truth, cancellability, the negation test, stress placement;
  3. have a sense of the field's history, from Frege and the logical positivists through ordinary language philosophy to Grice, contemporary cognitive science, Bayesian and information theory;
  4. compare four contemporary programmes (Neo-Gricean, Relevance Theory, Rational Speech Acts, Grammaticalism) and evaluate them against shared data;
  5. design and run a small empirical study of a pragmatic phenomenon.
