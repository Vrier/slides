Module Overview

Linguistic Pragmatics

01

What the module is about

People rarely say exactly what they mean. Hearers work out the rest, reliably, mostly without noticing. Pragmatics studies that: the route from what is said to what is meant. Semantics: what the words encode. Pragmatics: what use and context add. The boundary between the two is a live research question. It is where this course ends.

Two arcs. Weeks 1 to 8 cover the phenomena: speech acts, implicature, presupposition, common ground, information, politeness. For each: what did they mean, and how did we know? Weeks 9 to 12 introduce four live programmes: Neo-Gricean, Relevance Theory, Rational Speech Acts, Grammaticalism. Same data, different machinery. Every teaching week runs four strands: empirical → historical → formal → applied.

Learning outcomes

On successful completion of this module, students will be able to:

  1. identify and analyse the core phenomena (speech acts, implicature, presupposition, common ground and accommodation, information structure, politeness) in real language use;
  2. run the field's diagnostics: felicity vs truth, cancellability, the negation test, stress placement;
  3. trace the field's history, from Frege and the logical positivists through ordinary language philosophy to Grice;
  4. compare the four contemporary programmes (Neo-Gricean, Relevance Theory, Rational Speech Acts, Grammaticalism) and evaluate them against shared data;
  5. design and run a small empirical study of a pragmatic phenomenon in a chosen speech event, written up in research paper style.

03

Assessment

Research project · 100%

One assessment. A research project, written up at 3,500 to 4,000 words, in research paper style. Built across the term, week by week.

Choose: a phenomenon (implicature, presupposition, politeness…) and a setting, a speech event where it lives. Collect: a small dataset of real use. Transcripts, excerpts, a small corpus. Real examples only. Analyse & write: try each week's tools as they arrive.

Deadline: Monday 14 December 2026. Submit via Blackboard.

Weekly exercises

A sheet each teaching week. Formative. Carries no marks. Strongly recommended, and model answers follow. Good training for the project: several ask you to collect and analyse real data.
