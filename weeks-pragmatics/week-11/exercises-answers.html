Rational Speech Acts (RSA)

0

The model

Definitions
Reference gameA speaker has one of several people in mind and says one word. A listener picks the person she thinks he means.
State, sWhat the speaker has in mind; here, one of the people.
Utterance, uA word the speaker can use. The words he could have used in its place are its alternatives.
Meaning⟦u⟧(s): 1 if u is true of s, 0 if not.
PriorP(s): the listener’s belief in each state before anything is said. Flat: every state equally likely.

The speaker has one of these three people in mind. He can say hat, glasses or beard.

The pragmatic listener

Definitions
LikelihoodS1(u | s): how often a speaker with s in mind says u. The column for u in the speaker’s table.
EvidenceP(u): how often u is said at all. Likelihood times prior, added up over every state.
Pragmatic listenerL1(s | u): her belief in s after hearing u, reasoning about the speaker. The posterior of Bayes’ rule, with the states as hypotheses and the word as evidence.

Scalar implicature

Definitions
StatesThe number of red apples in a bowl of three: 0, 1, 2 or 3.
Utterancesnone, true of 0; some, true of 1, 2 and 3; all, true of 3.
Scalar implicatureThe inference from a weaker word to the denial of a stronger one, as from some to not all. In RSA, a shift of belief away from 3, with a strength.

Utility, cost and α

Definitions
Bits still neededlog2 1L0(s | u): the literal listener’s surprisal for s after hearing u. 0 when she is certain.
CostC(u): the effort of saying u, in bits. 0 unless stated.
UtilityU(u; s): how good u is for a speaker with s in mind. Minus the bits still needed, minus the cost.
Score2α · U(u; s). With no costs and α = 1 it is L0(s | u).
Rationality, αHow strongly the speaker prefers the better word. At 0 he picks among the true words at random; at 1 he is the speaker of section 2; as α grows he picks the best word more and more often.

6

What you need to know

Concepts
  1. Reference games; states, utterances and literal meanings.
  2. The prior; the literal listener.
  3. The speaker’s utility; informativity and cost; the parameter α.
  4. The pragmatic listener; levels of reasoning.
  5. Graded implicature; scalar implicature as a shift of belief.
  6. Hyperbole, politeness and overinformative reference in RSA.
  7. RSA beside Grice, the neo-Griceans and Relevance Theory.
Skills
  1. Set up a model from an example, with states, alternatives, a truth table and a prior.
  2. Compute a literal listener, a speaker at α = 1 and a pragmatic listener by hand.
  3. Derive a scalar implicature with numbers, and say how the prior, cost and α change it.
  4. Read L0, S1, L1, α and C in the literature.
  5. Apply RSA to an utterance from real data, stating the states and alternatives assumed.
  6. Compare an RSA analysis of an example with a Gricean, neo-Gricean or relevance-theoretic one.
