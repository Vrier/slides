Week 8 · Readings

Politeness

Reading list Figures: Goffman · Lakoff · Leech · Brown & Levinson · Culpeper

Five sources for Week 8: a textbook chapter, two classical works, on politeness strategies and on impoliteness, and two contemporary ones, on British and American politeness and on politeness as relational work. References are in APA; open-access links are given where available. Confirm editions and availability against the library / Blackboard list before circulating.

Reading list one textbook · two classical · two contemporary
  1. TextbookGrundy, P. (2008). Politeness phenomena. In Doing pragmatics (3rd ed., pp. 186–210). Hodder Education.
    Politeness at textbook level, with plenty of examples.
    Purchase
  2. ClassicalBrown, P., & Levinson, S. C. (1987). Politeness: Some universals in language usage (§§1–3). Cambridge University Press.
    Face, face-threatening acts and the politeness strategies.
    Purchase
  3. ClassicalCulpeper, J. (1996). Towards an anatomy of impoliteness. Journal of Pragmatics, 25(3), 349–367. https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-2166(95)00014-3
    Impoliteness strategies, built as the mirror image of the politeness strategies.
    Library
  4. ContemporaryMurphy, M. L. (2018). The prodigal tongue: The love–hate relationship between American and British English. Oneworld.
    British and American politeness and indirectness compared.
    Purchase
  5. ContemporaryLocher, M. A., & Watts, R. J. (2005). Politeness theory and relational work. Journal of Politeness Research, 1(1), 9–33. https://doi.org/10.1515/jplr.2005.1.1.9
    Politeness as relational work, judged in context rather than built into forms.
    Library
