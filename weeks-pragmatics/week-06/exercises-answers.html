Probability Theory Basics

Definitions
Probability distributionA function P, which gives each outcome a number from 0 to 1, with the numbers for all the outcomes adding up to 1.
P(x)The probability of outcome x.
Conditional probabilityP(B | A), the probability of B given A: the share of A taken up by B. P(A and B) = P(A) · P(B | A).
H, EH is the hypothesis, the claim you want to know about. E is the evidence, what you have observed.
PriorP(H): how probable the hypothesis was before the evidence.
LikelihoodP(E | H): how expected the evidence is if the hypothesis is true.
EvidenceP(E): how expected the evidence is overall. It is the products of prior and likelihood added up.
PosteriorP(H | E): how probable the hypothesis is once you have seen the evidence.
Bayes’ ruleP(H | E)posterior = likelihoodP(E | H) · priorP(H)P(E)evidence

Information Theory Basics

Definitions
BitThe basic unit of information. log2 10.5, the amount of information needed to eliminate half of the options.
Logarithmlog2 m = n iff 2n = m. log2 m counts the halvings that bring m down to 1.
A helpful equivalence for calculatorslog2 x = log10 xlog10 2
Surprisallog2 1P(x), in bits: the number of times you halve the possibilities to reach x.
Bits in an answerIts surprisal, log2 1P, where P is the probability, before asking, that the answer comes out that way.
Expected information gainWhat a yes/no question is worth before the answer is heard: P(yes) · log2 1P(yes) + P(no) · log2 1P(no).

Entropy, Compression, and Redundancy

Definitions
From countsP(x) = number of times x occurstotal
EventA set of outcomes, such as the speaker referring to something blue. Its probability is the sum of the probabilities of the outcomes in it.
Function wordA word that marks grammatical relations, such as the, of, and, in.
Content wordA word with lexical meaning, such as house, run, dog, beautiful.
Zipf’s law of abbreviationThe more often a word is used, the shorter it tends to be.
EntropyThe average surprisal of a distribution: H(X) = Σx P(x) · log2 1P(x). A property of the whole distribution; surprisal is a property of one outcome.
X, ΣxX: the outcomes, with distribution P. Σx adds up the expression after it once for every outcome x.
Maximum entropylog2 N for N outcomes, reached when the distribution is flat: all N equally likely. A certain outcome gives 0.
RedundancySignal beyond what the message strictly needs. Redundancy = 1 − HHmax.
HmaxThe entropy if every symbol were equally likely: log2 N for N symbols.
