Week 6 · Readings

Information

Reading list Figures: Bayes · Markov · Shannon · Zipf

Four sources for Week 6: a textbook introduction drawn as pictures, the classical paper that started information theory, and two contemporary papers on information and the shape of language. References are in APA; open-access links are given where available. Confirm editions and availability against the library / Blackboard list before circulating.

Reading list one textbook · one classical · two contemporary
  1. TextbookOlah, C. (2015). Visual information theory [Blog post]. colah’s blog.
    Probability, entropy and codes drawn as pictures; the source of the weather and clothing example.
    Open access Open page
  2. ClassicalShannon, C. E. (1948). A mathematical theory of communication. Bell System Technical Journal, 27(3), 379–423. https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1948.tb01338.x
    The founding paper of information theory. Part I is enough for this week.
    Open access Open PDF
  3. ContemporaryGibson, E., Futrell, R., Piantadosi, S. T., Dautriche, I., Mahowald, K., Bergen, L., & Levy, R. (2019). How efficiency shapes human language. Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 23(5), 389–407. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2019.02.003
    How the pressure to communicate efficiently shapes the lexicon, syntax and word order.
    Library
  4. ContemporaryFutrell, R., & Hahn, M. (2022). Information theory as a bridge between language function and language form. Frontiers in Communication, 7, 657725. https://doi.org/10.3389/fcomm.2022.657725
    Information theory as a link between what language is for and the forms it takes.
    Open access Open page
