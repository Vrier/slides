What the module is about

Pragmatics is the study of meaning in context: how speakers use language to communicate, and how hearers interpret what they say. Its central observation is that the meaning conveyed by an utterance regularly goes beyond the conventional meaning of the sentence uttered. Can you pass the salt? is not a question about ability. Some of the students passed conveys that not all did. The additional content is recovered from the context of use and from general principles of communication. Semantics studies the conventional meaning of expressions; pragmatics studies the contribution of use and context. The boundary between the two is a live research question.

Weeks 1 to 8 cover some core pragmatic phenomena: speech acts, implicature, presupposition, common ground, information, politeness. Weeks 9 to 12 introduce four contemporary programmes in modern pragmatics: Neo-Gricean, Relevance Theory, Rational Speech Acts, Grammaticalism. No prior pragmatics is assumed.

Learning outcomes

On successful completion of this module, students will be able to:

  1. identify and analyse the core phenomena (speech acts, implicature, presupposition, common ground and accommodation, information structure, politeness) in real language use;
  2. run diagnostics: felicity vs truth, cancellability, the negation test, stress placement;
  3. have a sense of the field's history, from Frege and the logical positivists through ordinary language philosophy to Grice, contemporary cognitive science, Bayesian and information theory;
  4. compare four contemporary programmes (Neo-Gricean, Relevance Theory, Rational Speech Acts, Grammaticalism) and evaluate them against shared data;
  5. collect short stretches of real language use and analyse them with the module's tools.

Three components, 20% + 20% + 60%. All submitted via Blackboard. Titles, formats and briefs are released on Blackboard in advance of each deadline.

Assessment 1, 20%. Covers weeks 1 to 4: pragmatic meaning, speech acts, implicature, presupposition. Due TBC (week 5). Feedback returns over the study week.

Assessment 2, 20%. Covers weeks 5 to 8: common ground and projection, information, politeness. Due TBC (week 9).

Assessment 3, 60%. Covers the term, with emphasis on weeks 9 to 12: the four programmes, tested against shared data. Due TBC (assessment session, 19 to 30 April 2027).

Weekly homework. A sheet each teaching week. Formative: it carries no marks, and model answers follow. Several sheets ask you to collect and analyse real data, which is the best preparation for the assessments.
